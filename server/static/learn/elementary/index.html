Rockets: elementary track

Eight 45-minute lessons with paper rockets. Built on the Washington State K–12 Science Learning Standards (the NGSS). Goes with Rocket Builder by Quiver Design Works (rocket.quiverdesignworks.net), used on a projector or tablets.

Students build and fly balloon, straw and stomp rockets, test fins and parachutes like engineers, and finish with a launch day. Rocket Builder is a class tool: the teacher (or a student "driver") designs rockets on the projector, shows the balance point and push point, and plays the simulated launch. A 3D-printed rocket can appear as an optional instructor demo. No motors for students: model rocket motors and kits are rated 12+.

Using Rocket Builder with grades 3–5

  • Open rocket.quiverdesignworks.net in Beginner mode on the projector. No account, download or install.
  • Build my rocket, then point at the blue dot (balance point) and the red dot (push point), and the stability score (green is good).
  • ▶ Watch it launch plays the flight: liftoff, push, coast, top, parachute, landing.
  • On tablets, groups can try their own designs. Keep it on screen: no downloads, prints or orders in this track.

Optional: the 3D-printed rocket as an instructor demo

  • Lesson 4, show-and-tell: a printed Rocket Builder rocket with no motor. Students hold it, find its balance point and compare it with the app.
  • Lesson 8, flight demo: only if an adult experienced with model rockets runs it under the NAR Model Rocket Safety Code, on a big enough field, with students behind a spectator line. Or visit a local NAR club launch. See the safety page.

Worksheets

The elementary worksheets (US Letter, big type, lots of pictures, little writing): E1–E8 (one per lesson), a parts-of-a-rocket page, the paper-rocket safety pledge, 45° sighting cards, a Rocket Scientist certificate and the answer key. Print at 100%.

Further resources

  • Apogee Components, Teach with Model Rockets: their free Rocketry Reservoir handout pack (parts of a rocket, flight phases, colouring pages, certificates) and lessons for grades 5–8, including The Optimum Descent Challenge (a parachute lesson). They are written for kit rockets with motors, so use the ideas, not the motor flights.
  • National Association of Rocketry: nar.org for the safety code and local club launches.

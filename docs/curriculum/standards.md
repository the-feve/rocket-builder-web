## Tools in Rocket Builder

- **Designer** (https://rocket.quiverdesignworks.net): Beginner and Advanced modes; the stability score with the blue CG and red CP dots; *How high will it go?*, *Which parachute?* and launch-field cards; **Nose weight (clay)** in Advanced. The ▾ download menu has a one-page **mission card** per design (`/mission/<build id>`), with the predicted flight, to print for launch day.
- **Launch simulator** (▶ Watch it launch, `/launch?build=<id>`): **Simple mode** for projectors (`&simple=1`), force arrows, flight graphs with **Download flight data (CSV)**, change-one-thing experiments, parachute vs streamer, a launch angle of 0–30°, and a **Predict, fly, compare** log (**Download log (CSV)**).
- **Altitude calculator** (https://rocket.quiverdesignworks.net/tools/altitude): height from an angle and a distance, with the working shown, the 45° rule and the average of two tracking stations.
- **Class codes** (https://rocket.quiverdesignworks.net/classes): the teacher signs in and makes a class code; students hand in designs with the code and a nickname, with no student accounts.

## Which science standards apply

- **In force now:** the **Washington State K–12 Science Learning Standards (WSSLS)**, adopted in 2013. They are the Next Generation Science Standards (NGSS) word for word, built on three dimensions: science and engineering practices (SEPs), disciplinary core ideas (DCIs) and crosscutting concepts (CCCs). Every code in these lessons is a 2013 WSSLS/NGSS performance expectation (PE).
- **Coming next:** OSPI published the **WA Science 2026** standards for initial adoption on 2026-10-15 (Bulletin 035-26). Final adoption is expected before June 2027; districts get the 2027–28 school year to prepare, and the standards must be taught from 2028–29. They stay grounded in the NGSS. The PEs used here keep their wording; only the codes change format (3-PS2-1 becomes **S.3.PS2.1**). WA Science 2026 also adds "key experience" tags (student-led investigations [INV], student-led engineering design [ENG], science outdoors [OTDR], climate learning [CLIM], environmental and sustainability education [ESE]) and makes Environmental and Sustainability Education a domain of its own.
- **State test:** the Washington Comprehensive Assessment of Science (WCAS) is given in grades 5, 8 and 11. The elementary track's PEs are in the grade 3–5 band tested in grade 5; the middle-school PEs are tested in grade 8.

## Science performance expectations used

The text is the official PE wording (identical in the 2013 standards and the WA Science 2026 draft). "Tags" and "Priority" are from the WA Science 2026 workbook.

| **3-PS2-2** | S.3.PS2.2 | Make observations and/or measurements of an object's motion to provide evidence that a pattern can be used to predict future motion. | | 3, 8 |
| **4-PS3-1** | S.4.PS3.1 | Use evidence to construct an explanation relating the speed of an object to the energy of that object. | | 8 |
| **5-PS2-1** | S.5.PS2.1 | Support an argument that the gravitational force exerted by Earth on objects is directed down. | | 2, 7 |
| **3-5-ETS1-1** | S.3-5.ETS1.1 | Define a simple design problem reflecting a need or a want that includes specified criteria for success and constraints on materials, time, or cost. | ENG | 5 |
| **3-5-ETS1-2** | S.3-5.ETS1.2 | Generate and compare multiple possible solutions to a problem based on how well each is likely to meet the criteria and constraints of the problem. | ENG | 5, 6 |

### Middle-school track (grades 6–8)

| PE | WA 2026 code | Official text | Tags | Lessons |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **MS-PS2-1** | S.MS.PS2.1 | Apply Newton's Third Law to design a solution to a problem involving the motion of two colliding objects. | ENG; priority | 1 (partial) |
| **MS-PS2-2** | S.MS.PS2.2 | Plan an investigation to provide evidence that the change in an object's motion depends on the sum of the forces on the object and the mass of the object. | INV, OTDR; priority | 1, 2, 3, 5, 7, 8 |
| **MS-PS2-4** | S.MS.PS2.4 | Construct and present arguments using evidence to support the claim that gravitational interactions are attractive and depend on the masses of interacting objects. | priority | 2 (connection) |

| **MS-PS3-5** | S.MS.PS3.5 | Construct, use, and present arguments to support the claim that when the kinetic energy of an object changes, energy is transferred to or from the object. | | 5 |
| **MS-ETS1-1** | S.MS.ETS1.1 | Define the criteria and constraints of a design problem with sufficient precision to ensure a successful solution, taking into account relevant scientific principles and potential impacts on people and the natural environment that may limit possible solutions. | ENG | 6, 8 |
| **MS-ETS1-2** | S.MS.ETS1.2 | Evaluate competing design solutions using a systematic process to determine how well they meet the criteria and constraints of the problem. | ENG | 4, 6 |
| **MS-ETS1-3** | S.MS.ETS1.3 | Analyze data from tests to determine similarities and differences among several design solutions to identify the best characteristics of each that can be combined into a new solution to better meet the criteria for success. | ENG | 3, 4, 7, 9 |

**Honest limits.** MS-PS2-1 is written about collisions; the rocket lessons apply the third law to a push-apart (rocket and exhaust), so they cover part of it. MS-PS2-4 and MS-PS3-2 are connections only. Fizzy rockets are an optional adult demo and are not mapped to the chemistry PEs (5-PS1-4, MS-PS1-2).

### Practices and crosscutting concepts

The SEP and CCC named in each lesson are the ones in that PE's NGSS foundation box, or, for the engineering PEs that list none, the one the activity uses most:

- **3-PS2-1, 3-PS2-2, 3-5-ETS1-3, MS-PS2-2:** planning and carrying out investigations. **5-PS2-1, MS-ETS1-2:** engaging in argument from evidence. **3-5-ETS1-1, MS-ETS1-1:** asking questions and defining problems. **3-5-ETS1-2, 4-PS3-1, MS-PS2-1:** constructing explanations and designing solutions. **MS-PS3-1, MS-ETS1-3:** analyzing and interpreting data. **MS-PS3-2, MS-ETS1-4:** developing and using models.
- **CCCs:** cause and effect (3-PS2-1, 5-PS2-1), patterns (3-PS2-2), energy and matter (4-PS3-1), systems and system models (MS-PS2-1, MS-PS3-2), stability and change (MS-PS2-2), scale, proportion and quantity (MS-PS3-1), influence of science, engineering and technology on society and the natural world (3-5-ETS1-1, 3-5-ETS1-2, MS-ETS1-1).

## Environment, sustainability and Since Time Immemorial

- **Environmental and Sustainability Education** is now a domain in WA Science 2026 (S.MS.ESE.1–3 at middle school). The rocket lessons don't claim an ESE standard. Middle-school Lesson 6 has an optional connection: MS-ETS1-1 asks for "impacts on people and the natural environment", so students add constraints about the launch site (fire risk, litter, wildlife) and plastic use in printing.
- **Since Time Immemorial** (OSPI's tribal sovereignty curriculum) isn't part of these lessons: rocketry has no natural link to it, and we didn't want to force one.

SSafety pledge

Lesson 1 · Read it together, then sign

I will keep everyone safe. I promise to:

  • wear safety glasses for straw and stomp rockets
  • never aim a rocket at a person, an animal or a window
  • wait for the countdown: 3, 2, 1, launch!
  • stand behind the line until the adult says “clear”
  • never chase a rocket into a road, a tree or a power line
  • never look at the sun
  • leave real rocket motors to adults (they are for ages 12+)

Signed

Model rocket motors and kits are rated 12+. In our class we fly paper and air rockets.

KAnswer keyTeachers

For teachers

E1 Balloon rocket

  • Data varies; the big balloon usually goes farther (a longer push).
  • The air pushes backward; the balloon goes forward. Held still: balanced.

E2 Straw rocket forces

  • Launch and going up: orange thrust arrow up, blue gravity arrow down (thrust longer while pushing). Coming down: blue gravity arrow down.
  • Gravity pulls down; it always came back down; apogee. Order: liftoff 1, coast 2, top 3, parachute 4.

E3 Launch angles

  • Data varies; about 45° (often a bit less) usually goes farthest; 75° goes shorter than 60°.
  • Pattern: the best angle is in the middle.

E4 Swing test

  • Usually A tumbles, B may wobble, C flies straight, D wobbles or tumbles.
  • The blue (balance point) dot is in front, nearer the nose. Fins work like the feathers on an arrow.

E5 Design plan

  • Any two different ideas; a reason that uses the criteria.

E6 Test and improve

  • Common failure points: a leaky nose, crooked or loose fins, fins too far forward. One change at a time.

E7 Parachute drop

  • Slowest: the big parachute. No parachute is fastest; the streamer is in between.
  • Bigger falls slower. Gravity pulls down; drag pushes up. Same washers: fair. Rocket Builder: often 12–18 inches.

E8 Launch log

  • Harder stomp: faster and higher, more energy. Above the 45° line from 10 m means higher than 10 m.

Parts of a rocket

  • Boxes left to right: nose cone, body tube, fins, parachute. Fins keep it flying straight; the parachute helps it land gently; the nose lets air flow smoothly around it.

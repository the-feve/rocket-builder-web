# Rockets for grades 3–5: the elementary track

_Eight 45-minute lessons with paper rockets. Built on the Washington State K–12 Science Learning Standards (the NGSS). Goes with **Rocket Builder by Quiver Design Works** (https://rocket.quiverdesignworks.net), used on a projector or tablets._

Students build and fly balloon, straw and stomp rockets, test fins and parachutes like engineers, and finish with a launch day. Rocket Builder is a class tool: the teacher (or a student "driver") designs rockets on the projector, shows the balance point and push point, and plays the simulated launch. A 3D-printed rocket can appear as an optional instructor demo. **No motors for students:** model rocket motors and kits are rated 12+.

## Lessons at a glance

| Lesson | Big idea | Hands-on | Rocket Builder | Worksheet |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Every push has a push back | Balloon rockets on a string | Optional: ▶ Watch it launch | E1 |
| 2 | Forces on a rocket; gravity pulls down | Build and fly straw rockets | Call out the parts of the flight | E2 |
| 3 | Patterns help us predict | Launch-angle test with a squeeze launcher | | E3 |
| 4 | Fins and balance | Swing test: no fins, fins, clay in the nose | Find the blue and red dots | E4 |
| 5 | Criteria and constraints | Design and build a stomp rocket | Compare two designs | E5 |
| 6 | Test, find the failure, improve | Stomp-rocket fair tests | Optional: change one thing, build | E6 |
| 7 | Parachutes and gravity | Parachute drop test | Which parachute? card | E7 |
| 8 | Launch day | Stomp launches, 45° tracking, parachute rocket | Compare with the app's predicted height | E8, 45° card, certificate |

**Short on time?** Lessons 1, 2, 4, 7 and 8 make a 5-lesson unit. Lessons 5 and 6 can be one longer block (about 90 minutes).

## Using Rocket Builder with grades 3–5

- Open https://rocket.quiverdesignworks.net in **Beginner** mode on the projector. No account, download or install.
- **Build my rocket**, then point at the **blue dot** (balance point) and the **red dot** (push point), and the stability score (green is good).
- **▶ Watch it launch** plays the flight: liftoff, push, coast, top, parachute, landing.
- On tablets, groups can try their own designs. Keep it on screen: no downloads, prints or orders in this track.

## Optional: the 3D-printed rocket as an instructor demo

- **Lesson 4, show-and-tell:** a printed Rocket Builder rocket with no motor. Students hold it, find its balance point and compare it with the app.
- **Lesson 8, flight demo:** only if an adult experienced with model rockets runs it under the NAR Model Rocket Safety Code, on a big enough field, with students behind a spectator line. Or visit a local NAR club launch. See the [safety page](safety.md).

## Worksheets

The [elementary worksheets](worksheets.html) (US Letter, big type, lots of pictures, little writing): E1–E8 (one per lesson), a parts-of-a-rocket page, the paper-rocket safety pledge, 45° sighting cards, a Rocket Scientist certificate and the answer key. Print at 100%.

## Further resources

- Apogee Components, [Teach with Model Rockets](https://www.apogeerockets.com/Teach-with-Model-Rockets): their free *Rocketry Reservoir* handout pack (parts of a rocket, flight phases, colouring pages, certificates) and lessons for grades 5–8, including *The Optimum Descent Challenge* (a parachute lesson). They are written for kit rockets with motors, so use the ideas, not the motor flights.
- National Association of Rocketry: [nar.org](https://www.nar.org/) for the safety code and local club launches.

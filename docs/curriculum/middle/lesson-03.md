# Lesson 3: Stability: the balance point and the push point

**Time:** about 60 minutes. **Worksheet:** W3 Swing test. **Rocket Builder:** Beginner mode.

## Standards

| Code | In plain words |
|---|---|
| **MS-ETS1-3** | Analyze test data from several designs to find the best features of each. |
| **MS-PS2-2** | Forces (here, the air on the fins) change an object's motion. |
| **6.EE.A.2** (math) | Evaluate a formula: score = (CP − CG) ÷ body width. |

**Practice:** analyzing and interpreting data. **Crosscutting concept:** stability and change.

## I can…

- I can find a rocket's balance point (CG) and estimate its push point (CP).
- I can explain why the CG must be in front of the CP.
- I can work out a stability score in calibers and say whether it's safe (1–2 cal is ideal).

## Words to know

- **Centre of gravity (CG), balance point**: Rocket Builder's **blue dot**.
- **Centre of pressure (CP), push point**: where the air's push acts on average. The **red dot**.
- **Caliber (cal)**: one body width, used as a ruler.
- **Stability margin (score)**: how far the CP is behind the CG, in calibers.

## Materials

Paper-towel tube, cardstock nose and fins, modeling clay, 1.5 m of string, scrap cardboard, a ruler, W3.

## Steps

### 1. Teach (10 minutes)

> Fins are a rocket's feathers. If a rocket tilts, air hits its side. When the push point is **behind** the balance point, the air swings the tail back in line, like a weathervane. If the push point is in front, the air swings the nose the wrong way and the rocket tumbles.
>
> Rocket Builder's score: **under 1 cal** too wobbly (no download), **1–2** ideal, **2–3** fine, **over 3** so stable it turns into the wind. The app checks it with the heaviest motor loaded, the worst case. To fix a low score: bigger or swept fins, a longer body, or nose weight.

### 2. Measure (15 minutes)

<!-- figure: cg-cp -->

1. Build a test rocket (tube, cone nose, 3–4 fins). Balance it on a finger: mark the CG (blue). Measure from the nose tip.
2. Trace the rocket's side view on cardboard, cut it out and balance the cut-out: that's a cautious estimate of the CP (red). Mark it.
3. Score = (CP − CG) ÷ body width.

### 3. Swing-test demo (20 minutes)

<!-- figure: swing-test -->

<!-- figure: rocket-versions -->

Outdoors, nobody within 3 m. Tie the string at the CG; swing the rocket in a circle; does the nose lead? Test **A** no fins, **B** fins, **C** fins + nose clay, **D** fins + tail clay. Re-find the CG each time. Record on W3.

### 4. Rocket Builder (10 minutes)

Build a rocket. Find the blue and red dots and the score. Change only the fin shape and rebuild. Which fins give the highest score?

### 5. Wrap up (5 minutes)

Which version was best, and what did the data show about why?

## Check for understanding

1. Which must be in front: CG or CP?
2. Body 2 cm wide; CP is 3 cm behind the CG. Score?
3. Is 0.5 cal OK to fly?
4. Name two ways to raise the score.

**Answer key**

1. The CG (blue) in front of the CP (red).
2. 3 ÷ 2 = **1.5 cal**.
3. No: wobbly; Rocket Builder won't let you download it.
4. Bigger or swept-back fins, a longer body, weight in the nose.

## Teacher notes

- **The swing test is a demonstration, not proof.** It can give wrong answers (string position, swing speed). For the real rocket, the go/no-go check is the balance point: with a motor in, the measured CG must sit in front of Rocket Builder's CP by at least one body width (Lesson 8).
- **What to expect:** A tumbles, C is steady, D wobbles or tumbles.
- **Extension:** a Rocket Builder rocket with CG 253 mm and CP 289 mm from the tip, body 21.4 mm: (289 − 253) ÷ 21.4 ≈ **1.7 cal**. Look up the Barrowman method, the hand calculation the app uses.
- **Further resources:** Apogee Components' free lesson *Fins and Finish Lines* (apogeerockets.com/downloads/PDFs/58-3.pdf).
- **Nose weight setting:** Advanced mode has **Nose weight (clay)**, in grams. Add 5 g at a time and rebuild: the CG moves forward and the score rises, but the predicted altitude drops. A good trade-off discussion.

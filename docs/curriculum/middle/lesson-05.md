# Lesson 5: Motors, impulse and energy

**Time:** about 60 minutes. **Worksheet:** W5 Motor codes and impulse (W5b thrust curve for extension). **Rocket Builder:** Beginner mode, *How high will it go?*

## Standards

| Code | In plain words |
|---|---|
| **MS-PS2-2** | A change in motion depends on the total force and the mass (thrust-to-weight, F = m × a). |
| **MS-PS3-5** | When an object's kinetic energy changes, energy was transferred to or from it. |
| **MS-PS3-2** (connection) | Higher up means more gravitational potential energy stored in the Earth–rocket system. |
| **6.RP.A.3, 6.EE.A.2** (math) | Ratios; evaluate impulse = average thrust × time. |

**Practice:** using mathematics and computational thinking. **Crosscutting concept:** energy and matter.

## I can…

- I can name the parts of a model rocket motor and what each does.
- I can decode a motor code like **C6-5** and calculate total impulse.
- I can trace energy through a flight: stored in the propellant, moving (kinetic), height (potential), and lost to drag as heat.

## Words to know

- **Propellant, nozzle, delay, ejection charge**: the parts of a motor, bottom to top.
- **Igniter**: the wire that lights the motor. Adults only.
- **Total impulse (N·s)**: average thrust × burn time; the class letter (A, B, C…) gives its range. Each letter is up to twice the one before.
- **Kinetic energy**: energy of motion. **Gravitational potential energy**: energy stored by height.

## Materials

Calculators, W5. Optional: a sealed motor pack (adult shows it, unopened). Optional adult-led demo: film canisters with inside-snap lids, effervescent tablets, water, safety glasses.

## Steps

### 1. Teach: inside a motor (10 minutes)

> A model rocket motor is a factory-made paper tube: a **nozzle**, **propellant** that burns for about a second, a **delay** that makes a smoke trail while the rocket coasts, and an **ejection charge** that pops the nose and pushes out the parachute near apogee. Only adults handle motors and igniters.
>
> In **C6-5**: C is the impulse class (5.01–10 N·s), 6 is the average thrust in newtons, 5 is the delay in seconds.

### 2. Decode and calculate (20 minutes)

Work through W5: decode four codes, find impulse = average thrust × burn time and the class, and check thrust-to-weight (100 g weighs about 1 N; it must be 5 or more).

### 3. Energy through a flight (15 minutes)

<!-- figure: energy-path -->

Draw a flight path. Label where energy is:

- **On the pad:** stored in the propellant (chemical).
- **Boost:** the motor transfers energy to the rocket: its speed and height grow, so its kinetic and potential energy grow.
- **Coast:** kinetic energy turns into potential energy as it slows and climbs; drag turns some into heat in the air.
- **Apogee:** the most potential energy, almost no kinetic.
- **Descent:** the parachute's drag takes energy away, so it lands slowly.

### 4. Rocket Builder (10 minutes)

Build your rocket. Find **How high will it go?**: the recommended motor and predicted altitude. Use the speed and altitude there for the energy story.

### 5. Wrap up (5 minutes)

Why do bigger motors need bigger fields?

## Check for understanding

1. In B6-4, what do the B, 6 and 4 mean?
2. 5 N average for 2 s: total impulse and class?
3. Rocket + motor weighs 1.2 N; average thrust 5 N. Pass the 5× rule?
4. During the coast the rocket slows down. Where does its kinetic energy go?
5. Who puts the igniter in the motor?

**Answer key**

1. Class B (2.51–5 N·s), about 6 N average thrust, 4 s delay.
2. 10 N·s, class C.
3. No: 5 ÷ 1.2 ≈ 4.2.
4. Mostly into gravitational potential energy (height); some into heat through drag.
5. An adult. Always.

## Teacher notes

- **Motor classes and NAR minimum fields:** 1/2A and smaller 15 m (50 ft); A 30 m; B 60 m; C 120 m; D 150 m; E–F 300 m. Typical printed rockets: M13 15–150 m high, M18 60–300 m, M24 150–350 m, M29 300–750 m.
- **Optional adult-led fizzy demo (third law and energy from a reaction):** an adult drops half a tablet into a film canister with a teaspoon of water, snaps the lid on, sets it lid-down and steps back; outside, glasses on, nobody leans over it; if it doesn't pop, wait 30 s. The adult keeps the tablets (Alka-Seltzer contains aspirin).
- **Extension (W5b):** plot the B-motor thrust curve and find the area under it (≈ 4.3 N·s). F = m × a at liftoff: 0.086 kg rocket, 4.7 N average thrust: weight ≈ 0.84 N, net ≈ 3.9 N, a ≈ 45 m/s². Kinetic energy at 50 m/s: ½ × 0.086 × 50² ≈ 108 J; potential energy at 120 m: 0.086 × 9.8 × 120 ≈ 101 J. Where did the other energy go? (Drag.)
- **Students under 12** do the paper and math parts; the motor itself stays with an adult.
- **Energy from real numbers:** the launch simulator's flight graphs show speed and altitude against time, and **Download flight data (CSV)** gives the numbers. Students compute kinetic energy (½mv²) and potential energy (mgh) at a few moments in a spreadsheet and graph both.

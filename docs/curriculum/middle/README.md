# Rockets for grades 6–8: the middle-school track

_Nine lessons, about 55–60 minutes each, plus a launch day. Built on the Washington State K–12 Science Learning Standards (the NGSS). Goes with **Rocket Builder by Quiver Design Works** (https://rocket.quiverdesignworks.net), in Beginner mode first and Advanced mode from Lesson 6._

Students investigate forces, stability, drag, motors and energy with paper rockets, then design a rocket in Rocket Builder, 3D-print it (or order it printed) and fly it on a real model rocket motor. **Adults handle all motors and igniters**, every time, under the NAR Model Rocket Safety Code. Motor flights and kits are rated **12+**: students under 12 (some 6th graders) do every lesson and take launch-day jobs (tracker, recorder, countdown caller), and keep their designs on screen.

## Lessons at a glance

| Lesson | Big idea | Hands-on | Rocket Builder | Worksheet |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Newton's three laws | Balloon rocket: more mass, same push | First look, ▶ Watch it launch | W1 |
| 2 | Forces in flight | Force diagrams; straw rockets and launch angle | Flight phases; power bar (thrust ÷ weight) | W2 |
| 3 | Stability: CG and CP | Balance point, cut-out CP, swing-test demo | Blue and red dots, fin shapes | W3 |
| 4 | Drag and shape | Nose-cone and fin tests | Nose shapes (Advanced: drag coefficient) | W4 |
| 5 | Motors, impulse and energy | Motor codes, impulse math, energy story | *How high will it go?* | W5, W5b |
| 6 | Designing with a model | One-variable design log, decision table | Beginner, then Advanced custom fins; save; 12+ download | W6 |
| 7 | Parachutes and landing energy | Drop test; kinetic energy graphs | *Which parachute?*, drift | W7 |
| 8 | Launch prep | NAR code, misfire drill, inclinometer, preflight | *How big a launch field?* | W8, T, W8b, W8c |
| 9 | Launch day and mission report | Adult-run launch; tracking; report | Predicted altitude | F, W9, C |

**Plan ahead (adult):** order the pad and controller, wadding and motors in Lesson 1; find a field (or a local NAR club launch) by Lesson 5; start printing in Lesson 6 (several hours per part); assemble with an adult before Lesson 8; pick a backup launch day.

**Short on time?** Lessons 1+2, 3+4 and 7+8 combine into single long blocks (keep the balloon rocket, the swing test demo, the nose-cone test, the drop test and the inclinometer).

## Rocket Builder: Beginner, then Advanced

- **Lessons 1–5, Beginner:** the tutorial walks through motor size, printer height, rocket height, fins and nose. Students read the stability score, the blue (CG) and red (CP) dots, the power bar, *How high will it go?* and *Which parachute?*, and press **▶ Watch it launch**.
- **Lesson 6 on, Advanced:** custom fin size, drag coefficient, wall thickness and the full report with key dimensions (CG and CP distances). The motor table under *Grown-up details* lists every motor for the size with a ✓ for those that pass.
- Saved designs keep their link; an account (adult, or 13+) keeps them across devices.

## A note on the numbers

They match Rocket Builder: stability 1–2 cal is ideal, under 1 is too wobbly to download, over 3 is over-stable; thrust at least **5 times** the weight; at least **15 m/s** off the rod; at least **30 m** high so the parachute can open; a parachute that brings the rocket down at about **3–5 m/s**. The app's numbers are estimates. Comparing them with the real flight is the point of Lesson 9.
